Before you pick

Pricing questions.

Which tier do I actually need?

If you already have brokerage accounts and don't mind clicking, Plays Only tells you what is splitting and when. If you want a machine to place and record the trade in one account, that's Automation Tool. If you hold accounts at several brokers — which is the entire point of the strategy — Mirror Automation is the one that matches how the trade actually works.

Do I need my own brokerage accounts?

Yes. RSAMAXXED is not a broker and never holds your money. It logs into accounts you already own, on your own machine, and places orders you authorize. You open the accounts; we automate the clicking.

Does the price scale with how much I make?

No. It's a flat monthly fee and we take no cut of any trade. We never see your balances — only the trade journal you choose to sync, and only if you pair the dashboard.

What if a broker changes its policy or breaks the integration?

Broker modules break; unofficial interfaces change without warning. We fix them, but we can't promise a given broker works on a given day, and we can't make a broker round your fraction up. Read what can go wrong before you subscribe — it is not a short list.

Can I switch tiers or cancel?

Switch up or down whenever you like; the change takes effect on your next billing date. Cancelling stops the next charge and leaves your trade history exportable as CSV.

Which platforms does the desktop terminal run on?

Windows today. The broker automation leans on a native browser stack, so macOS and Linux are not supported yet. Plays Only works in any browser.

Still deciding?

Read the mechanism first. The pricing makes more sense once the trade does.

How it works

RSAMAXXED is trading automation software, not a broker, adviser, or fiduciary. This is not investment advice and not a solicitation. Subscription fees buy access to software and an alert feed — not a trading result. The $4.75 and $47.50 figures above assume one $0.25 share per account and a 1-for-20 split whose fraction rounds up: they are the arithmetic of a hypothetical split, illustrative only, never a forecast, a promise, or past performance. Round-up treatment varies by broker and by corporate action, changes without notice, and may not happen at all: you can lose money.
